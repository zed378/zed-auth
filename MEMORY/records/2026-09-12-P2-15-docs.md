## "Byte for byte" is a testable claim, so it is tested

The Definition of Done asks that *the claim format in the docs matches the emitted tokens
byte for byte*. That is a statement about two files agreeing, and reading them carefully
is the weakest possible way to establish it — a consumer team writes
`claims["urn:authservice:..."]` against what the page says, and a single-character
difference produces an integration that finds no roles and therefore denies everything,
silently, with no error anywhere.

So `backend/internal/oauth/token/docsclaims_test.go` walks the published pages and
asserts the exact strings this package emits appear in them, building the expected value
from `RoleClaimNamespace` rather than typing it out.

It was verified by introducing exactly the typo a reader would copy —
`org:project:` → `org:projects:` — in both pages that carry it. The test failed with the
sentence that explains why it matters. Restored, green.

The same file also guards the `MaxRoleClaims` bound (a number a consumer plans around, so
a page naming a different one is worse than a page naming none) and step 7's audit: no
page may describe Project Grants or ABAC as something you can do today without naming the
phase they arrive in.

`readDocs` refuses to run against fewer than five pages, because a docs folder that moved
would otherwise make every assertion pass against nothing.

## Three guides, and one of them argues against itself

- **Define roles and assign them** — the calls, and the three rules that follow from a
  grant referencing a role by key: the key cannot change, the role cannot be deleted while
  referenced, and you cannot grant to yourself.
- **Validate role claims in your service** — the exact claim, Go and TypeScript, and a
  verification checklist whose two most-skipped items (`aud` and `typ`) are called out as
  the difference between a check and the appearance of one.
- **Use `/v1/authz/check` for real-time decisions** — which exists largely to talk a
  reader **out** of trusting a token claim for anything irreversible, and to state the
  revocation window as a number rather than as the word "real-time".

That third guide is the one the card says a consumer can get wrong as a real security bug,
so it is explicit where it would be more comfortable to be reassuring:

> **If either happens, a revoked permission may still be honoured for up to 30 seconds.**
>
> That is the real bound. If you are building a step that cannot be undone, that is the
> window you are accepting.

And the failure mode: a `503` is not a denial. `allowed: false` means *we checked and the
answer is no* — cacheable, loggable as a policy decision. `503` means *nothing was
checked*. Conflating them makes an outage look like somebody's access was removed.

## A page that understated what shipped

Step 7 is about pages claiming too much. The audit found the opposite: `concepts/authorization.md`
still ended Layer 1 with *"Arrives in Phase 2."*

A page saying a shipped capability is coming is as wrong as one saying an unshipped
capability is here — it just fails safe. Removed, and the section filled out with what the
three concepts actually are (permission key, role, grant) and the manager-role hierarchy
as a table.
